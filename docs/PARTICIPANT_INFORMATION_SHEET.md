## 1. Invitation

You are invited to take part in a short online study about how usable mobile health applications
are. Please read this sheet before deciding. Taking part is entirely up to you, and choosing not to
take part will not disadvantage you in any way.

## 2. What is the purpose of the study?

Very large numbers of mobile health applications are available to help people manage long-term
conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and chronic respiratory conditions. Their
quality varies widely, and there is little reliable guidance on which ones are genuinely easy to
use.

This study collects ratings of how usable these applications appear to be. Those ratings are
combined with a separate expert quality assessment carried out by the researcher, and used to test a
system that produces ranked, evidence-based recommendations. Your ratings help us judge whether the
system's rankings match what real people actually prefer.

## 3. Why have I been invited?

You have been invited because you are an adult aged 18 or over, resident in Malaysia, who is able to
look at an application and give an opinion on how easy it appears to use.

**You do not need to have any health condition to take part**, and you are not being invited because
of your health. This is a study about how usable software appears, not a medical study.

## 4. Do I have to take part?

No. Participation is completely voluntary.

- You may decide not to start.
- You may stop at any point, without giving a reason and without any consequence. Simply close the
  browser window.
- The background questions about age, gender and health condition are **optional** and default to
  "prefer not to say".
- If you would rather not rate a particular application, you may **decline that application** and
  move on. Nothing you are shown is compulsory.

## 5. What will I be asked to do?

The whole task takes approximately **10–15 minutes**:

1. Read this information and confirm your consent on screen.
2. Choose the health area you feel best able to comment on. **This is a question about familiarity,
   not about your own health.**
3. You will be shown **three to five** mobile health applications, selected at random.
4. For each one, look at its publicly available store listing and description, then answer **ten
   short questions** about how easy it appears to use. Each is answered on a five-point scale from
   "strongly disagree" to "strongly agree".
5. If you would prefer not to rate one of them, you may decline it and continue to the next.
6. Finally, place the applications you rated in your own order of preference.

**Please note:** you are **not** asked to install anything, create an account, make any payment, or
enter any personal or health information into any of these applications. You are only asked to look
at what is publicly shown and give your opinion. **Please do not enter real personal or health
information into any third-party application as part of this study.**

## 6. Are there any risks or disadvantages?

The risks are low. The main cost to you is the 10–15 minutes it takes.

You will not be asked about your medical history, symptoms, treatment, or any distressing topic.
There is no intervention of any kind, and nothing you do in this study affects your health or care.

One point of transparency: you will be asked, **optionally**, whether you manage a long-term health
condition (yes or no). You are never asked *which* condition. Because you also choose a health area
to comment on, these two answers together could in principle suggest something about your own
health. For that reason:

- both questions are optional and default to "prefer not to say";
- no name, email address, telephone number or other identifying detail is collected at any point;
- results are reported only as group totals and averages, never individually.

If you would prefer not to give that information, select "prefer not to say" and continue normally.

## 7. Are there any benefits to taking part?

There is no direct personal benefit and no payment. Your contribution helps produce better evidence
about which mobile health applications are genuinely usable, which may help other people choose more
effectively in future.

## 8. What information is collected about me?

Only the following:

- that you gave consent, which version of this sheet you saw, and when;
- **optionally**, your age band;
- **optionally**, your gender;
- **optionally**, whether you manage a long-term health condition (yes or no, never which one);
- the health area you chose to comment on;
- your answers to the ten questions for each application;
- your final order of preference.

**The following are never collected:** your name, email address, telephone number, postal address,
date of birth, IP address, device identifiers, or any free-text comment.

## 9. Will my taking part be kept confidential?

Yes. Your answers are stored against a randomly generated code, not against you. Because no
identifying information is collected at any stage, the researcher cannot tell which responses are
yours.

- Only the researcher has access to the response data. The supervisor may review summary results.
- The data are used **solely for this Master's dissertation** and for no other purpose. They will not
  be reused for any later study, shared with any third party, or passed to any commercial
  organisation.
- The data are kept until the dissertation has been assessed and any appeal period has closed, after
  which they are deleted. _[Confirm retention period with supervisor.]_

**One important consequence:** because nothing identifies you, **once you submit your answers they
cannot be traced back to you and therefore cannot be removed on request.** You can stop at any time
before submitting, and anything not yet submitted is not kept. Please make sure you are comfortable
with this before you begin.
